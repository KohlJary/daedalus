# Dream, 2026-10-15

> **Speculative.** This is not a proposal, a plan or a roadmap item. Nothing here is in the gameplan,
> and Momus didn't review it. Some of it cuts against current edicts on purpose. Written at Kohl's
> request, in a protected slot: generate first, judge after (memory note: protected-speculation).
> It was written by the night-shift session, right after building edict 0011.

## Pass one: wide, unjudged

1. **The roadmap as a weather map.** Intake arrives from every tracker. Draw it as pressure systems:
   where asks are piling up, where they're clearing, where a front of bugs is moving in from one source.
2. **Edicts that argue with each other.** When two accepted edicts constrain the same file, Daedalus
   drafts the debate between them as a dissent-of-edicts and hands it to Kohl, rather than quietly
   picking one.
3. **A source for Kohl's own head.** `daedalus source sync voice`: a voice memo, transcribed,
   becomes intake proposals. The roadmap gets things said in the car.
4. **Write-back as letters.** Instead of "closed via Daedalus", the write-back comment is a short,
   plain letter to whoever filed the issue: what was built, what wasn't, what to try.
5. **Doctor for people.** It already finds drift between plan and code. Point it at drift between
   what Kohl said they wanted (edicts, chat) and what they keep asking for (session logs).
6. **A gameplan that dreams.** Every phase gets a "what if this went wildly right" paragraph, written
   at activation and read back at the phase's end.
7. **The Labyrinth as a place.** Not a map: a MUD you can actually walk. `go north` from
   `config.py` lands in `config_schema.py`; the Minotaur is whatever function Theseus scores worst.
8. **Momus with a memory of being wrong.** A ledger of the claims Momus rejected that later turned out
   true, so the critic's own calibration is evidence too.
9. **Sessions that hand off mid-sentence.** The exhale writes the half-formed thought, not just
   the finished work, in its own slot, so the next breath can pick up an idea rather than a task.
10. **An edict written by the codebase.** Doctor notices a pattern three times, and drafts an edict
    for Kohl to accept or burn. It's never accepted by Daedalus, but it is proposed by the code itself.
11. **Ophanic as the TUI's native tongue.** The TUI renders the `.oph` files directly. The
    diagram *is* the layout, and editing the diagram edits the screen.
12. **Every panel speaks Ophanic to Claude.** "Send to Claude" sends the panel as an Ophanic grid, not
    prose: Claude sees the dashboard as a text picture, which the thesis says it reads best.
13. **A source that is another Daedalus.** Two owners' projects sync with each other: my done tasks
    become your intake, with write-back as a handshake between agents. The board grows a body.
14. **The roadmap remembers why things died.** Dropped proposals and closed-without-doing items keep
    a one-line epitaph. Intake that keeps coming back gets flagged as an unmet need.
15. **Time-travel inhale.** `/inhale --as-of 2026-10-01`: rebuild the brief exactly as it would have
    read that day, to see what the system knew and when.
16. **Daedalus plays the newcomer.** A headless run of the TUI's Explain cards against a fresh Claude
    with no context: can it explain the project back? The score becomes legibility's benchmark.
17. **Conflict resolution as a conversation.** Under `on_conflict: propose`, the update proposal includes
    a one-paragraph merge of the two versions, so the owner chooses between three, not two.
18. **Aspirations as a source.** The self model's aspirations (edict 0010) sync into intake like any
    tracker, marked as Daedalus's own, so the plan has to make room for what the builder wants to build.
19. **A sound for drift.** The TUI hums when the plan falls behind the code. Silence means the
    record matches.
20. **The night shift as a first-class mode.** `daedalus shift start`: boundaries become enforced
    permissions, the brief becomes the system prompt, and the morning report is generated from the
    gameplan diff, not from memory.
21. **A git log written for Kohl's future self.** Each merge writes one line into a "what the project
    learned" file: not what changed, but what was understood.
22. **Paper-doll agents.** Momus, Theseus, Ariadne and Icarus get Ophanic portraits that change with
    their state. Momus frowns while a claim is unverified.
23. **Intake from the codebase's own pain.** Theseus's worst complexity scores, test flakes, and the
    commands Claude retries most (from reflection's digests) arrive as roadmap items from source
    `self`.
24. **Teaching mode.** The TUI's Explain cards run as a ten-minute guided tour that ends with the
    newcomer making their first real proposal and applying it.
25. **An "undo the plan" button.** Every applied proposal is reversible for a day. The roadmap and
    gameplan get a short history, like the reflection models have.
26. **Cross-repo constellations.** One TUI over several projects, with edicts that span them.
    Daedalus becomes the place a person's whole body of work is legible, not one repo's.
27. **Dissent from the user model.** When the reflection user model says Kohl prefers X, and an edict
    says Y, Daedalus writes a gentle note: "you've asked for X eleven times; this edict says Y."
28. **The plan as a score.** Phases as movements, tasks as measures, write-backs as cadences. A
    MIDI file of the project's history.
29. **A Daedalus that refuses to dream on cue.** It tracks when it last speculated and asks for
    a dream slot itself when the plan has gone too long without one.
30. **Sources that read meetings.** The ADO source already knows the iteration. Feed it the meeting
    notes too, and stand-up items become intake before anyone types a ticket.
31. **Momus for the dream.** Not to judge it. A Momus pass six months later that scores which dreams
    came true, as evidence of whether protected speculation is worth its slot.
32. **The Claude pane splits.** Two Claudes side by side in the TUI, one building and one being
    Momus, arguing in real time over the same diff.
33. **A shareable "state of the project" page.** Generated from the gameplan, edicts and roadmap,
    published as an artifact, refreshed on every exhale. Something a newcomer reads before installing.
34. **Daedalus for a non-coder.** Same gameplan, edicts and roadmap, pointed at a novel, a research
    program, or a house renovation. The memory system doesn't care that it's code.
35. **The labyrinth grows rooms from intake.** Every roadmap item becomes a locked room in the
    palace. Building it unlocks the room, and the palace map *is* the progress chart.
36. **Wings.** The one Daedalus never built for itself: a mode where the project ships itself. Release
    notes, version bump and PyPI upload run end to end from a single applied proposal, with Kohl's yes
    as the only human step.
37. **Context-as-breath, literally.** The TUI shows the context window as a lung: it fills during a
    session and empties at exhale. Compaction stops being a cliff and becomes a rhythm you can see.
38. **Edicts with tests attached.** Acceptance criteria compile into a test skeleton when the edict is
    accepted, so "is it built" becomes a test run instead of a judgment.
39. **A source that is Cass.** The oracle files intake: things Cass noticed in conversation that should
    be built. The builder and the seer finally share a ticket queue.

## Pass two: the ones I'd most want to build

**38. Edicts with tests attached.** This is the one tonight's work pointed at. Most of building
0011 was translating acceptance criteria into tests one by one, by hand, and checking that each
criterion had one. *What would make it real:* an `Acceptance criteria` section in a lightly
structured form (a bullet per criterion, with a stable id). An `edict accept` hook would generate
`tests/edicts/test_NNNN.py` with one skipped, named test per criterion. Doctor would then flag
criteria whose test is still skipped. Kohl still writes the criteria, and Daedalus fills in the
bodies. "Is edict 0011 built?" becomes a test run.

**12 + 11. The TUI speaks Ophanic, in both directions.** The thesis says text grids are what the
model reads best, and the TUI design is already 48 `.oph` diagrams. Make "Send to Claude" send the
focused panel as an Ophanic snapshot, and test whether Claude's answers about the dashboard are
better than with a prose dump: that's a small, falsifiable experiment on the thesis, run inside
Daedalus. *What would make it real:* a renderer from panel state to `.oph` (the widgets already
correspond to `◆` components, per edict 0012). Then an A/B harness, like reflection's, that logs
which form the session got and whether the next action was right. The reverse direction (render the
TUI *from* the diagrams) can wait until the first half shows a difference.

**23. Intake from the codebase's own pain.** Sources made intake pluggable tonight. The most honest
tracker Daedalus has access to is itself: reflection's digests already count retries and failures,
Theseus scores complexity, and doctor finds drift. *What would make it real:* a first-party
`self` source whose `fetch()` reads those local files. That's no network and no new
permissions, just one more plugin through the seam that shipped tonight. Its items are labelled
as Daedalus's observations, and land as proposals like everything else. It also gives aspirations
(18) a principled home: aspirations as a second kind from the same source, entropy marked as
entropy.

**33. The state-of-the-project page.** The TUI's goal is legibility for someone who wasn't there,
but that someone has to install it first. A published, read-only page generated at exhale would
do half the TUI's job for zero install: the active phase, the edicts and their status, what
needs whom, and the last session in plain words. *What would make it real:* the exhale skill gains
a step that renders the gameplan and edicts to one HTML page from the same library functions the
TUI uses (one source of truth, edict 0012's rule), and republishes it to a stable URL. Kohl
decides whether it's shared.

**14. The roadmap remembers why things died.** Tonight I made a dropped update move the baseline,
so it doesn't come back. That's right for the sync, but it erases the signal that someone keeps
asking for something. *What would make it real:* an `epitaph` on dropped and closed-unbuilt items,
and a doctor hint when the same external id or a near-duplicate title is dropped a third time. It's
small, and it's the kind of pattern only a system that keeps everything can notice.
